📘 Circles, Projections, and Harmonic Motion

An original miniature textbook. A point travelling around a unit circle gives a geometric model of oscillation. Follow the sections in the sidebar; expand the proof and try the exercises.

📚 From a circle to a coordinate

🚩 Learning Objectives
Describe a circle using sine and cosine
Prove conservation of a quadratic energy
Recover amplitude from initial position and velocity

📖 Unit-circle projection
For an angle $t$ measured in radians, the point $(\cos t,\sin t)$ lies on the unit circle. Its horizontal projection is $x(t)=\cos t$.

🖋️ Circle identity
{{{
\cos^2 t+\sin^2 t=1
}}}

🧠 Two views of one motion
Uniform circular motion has constant speed. Its horizontal shadow slows near its turning points and speeds up near the origin.

❓ A quarter turn
At $t=0$ the projection is $1$. At $t=\pi/2$ it is $0$. At $t=\pi$ it is $-1$.

⚠️ Angles and derivatives
The derivative formulas here assume radians, not degrees.

📚 An oscillator and its energy

For unit mass and unit stiffness, Newton's equation is $x''+x=0$. Write $v=x'$.

🔬 Conserved energy
Every twice differentiable real solution of $x''+x=0$ has constant energy $E=(v^2+x^2)/2$.

🔏 Proof by differentiation
Differentiate along the solution:
$$\frac{dE}{dt}=v v'+x x'=v(-x)+xv=0.$$
Therefore $E$ is constant on every interval of the solution.

🖋️ Initial data and solution
{{{
x(t)=a\cos t+b\sin t,\qquad x(0)=a,\quad x'(0)=b
}}}

❓ Recovering amplitude
For $a=3$ and $b=4$, the amplitude is $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}=5$ and $E=25/2$.

💻 Evaluate the exact solution in JavaScript
{{{
function position(t, a, b) {
  return a * Math.cos(t) + b * Math.sin(t);
}
// 📘 Inside this fence the marker is literal text.
console.log(position(0, 3, 4)); // 3
}}}

💡 Compare the formulas
Differentiate the displayed solution twice and substitute into the oscillator equation.

📚 Practice and continuation

✏️ Practice Problems
Find the energy when $a=1$ and $b=0$.
Show that $v(t)=-a\sin t+b\cos t$.
Explain why the amplitude is zero exactly when $a=b=0$.

📝 Homework
For $x''+\omega^2x=0$ with $\omega>0$, derive the energy $(v^2+\omega^2x^2)/2$ and prove it is constant.
